The problem

Chargebacks spike every holiday season, but dispute-ops teams struggle to say which reason codes drove it, how much is friendly fraud, and whether they are winning or losing those disputes — let alone how their book tracks the wider market. This pack shows an agent grounded only in governed dispute data, benchmarked against real CFPB consumer complaints, with a verification step that catches anything unsupported — the difference between a toy and something a risk team will act on.

How it works

  1. Semantic view — governed business meaning over the dispute book (reason code, network, merchant category, month); Cortex Analyst answers natural-language questions against it.
  2. Fact pack — only the verified, pre-aggregated rows for each reason category; the agent's entire allowed universe of numbers.
  3. Grounded draft — the model writes the chargeback note under DATA RULES: copy figures exactly, no estimates, no hedging, no outside benchmarks.
  4. Verifier — a second pass checks every claim against the fact pack and flags the note Verified or Needs review.

The data story

A card payments processor's own dispute ledger has no public source, so DISPUTE_FACT is synthetic (per-dispute, holiday-skewed). The benchmark side is real: free CFPB consumer complaints for payment-card products. The two are bridged by month only — complaint counts are a market-wide signal, never summed with internal disputes. Example signals in the synthetic book: disputes rise ~60% in Nov–Jan, and the fraud codes (10.4 / 4837) carry the lowest win rates. Swap in your own chargeback ledger and the reason-code and win-rate math is unchanged.
